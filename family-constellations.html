About

Family Constellations

Family (Systemic) Constellations is a gentle but powerful way of seeing what is happening beneath the surface, so that real change becomes possible. It looks at the wider family system we belong to, and at the patterns that can pass quietly between generations.

How a constellation unfolds

After a short conversation about what you’d like to work on, I guide you in creating a “constellation”, a living map of the people or elements involved. In groups, other participants stand in as representatives. In one to one sessions we use objects or guided visualisation.

As the picture unfolds, what’s hidden often becomes clear surprisingly quickly.

What a constellation looks likeHow to read the picture above+

Imagine a room, or the picture above seen from overhead. The figure in the centre stands for the person, or the question, at the heart of the work. The others stand for the people and elements around it: parents, grandparents, a partner, someone who has been lost or left out of the family story, even a symptom or a goal.

Each figure has a position. How close or far away they stand, which way they face, and who they look towards or away from all carry meaning. The dotted lines show the connections between them. A figure turned away might be someone who has been excluded, or who has stepped back from the rest.

In a group, volunteers stand in as representatives and are placed intuitively. In one to one sessions we use objects, or a guided visualisation, to make the same map.

What the map revealsHow a picture can say what words cannot+

A constellation makes a family system visible in space. Dynamics that are hard to put into words often show up at once: who is carrying something that isn’t theirs, who is missing, who stands too close or too far away.

Representatives often report physical sensations or feelings that match the person they stand for, even when they know very little about them. I use what emerges to find what has been unspoken, and then to explore, gently, the movements and words that help each person find a more settled place.

Nothing is forced. You stay in charge of what feels true for you, and many people say that a single picture showed them what years of thinking had not.

Where it comes fromFamily systems therapy, psychodrama and older ideas of belonging+

Family Constellations grew out of several streams of thought. Family systems therapy showed that a person’s difficulties are often held within the wider family. Psychodrama and family sculpting showed that placing people in space can reveal what words alone cannot. And many traditions, in different cultures, have long held that we belong to those who came before us.

The method became widely known through the German therapist Bert Hellinger (1925–2019), who drew on these streams, including his years in South Africa working among Zulu communities.

The work I offer is centred on your own experience. You decide what feels true for you, and nothing is imposed on you.

Is it therapy?

No. Constellations is often described as the shortest of short term therapies: an intervention that can open new insight or movement. For complex or long-standing trauma it works best alongside longer term support.

Is it spiritual?

It isn’t connected to any one religion. The work draws on systemic and psychotherapeutic principles, and many people find it touches something spiritual. How you understand that is entirely up to you.

Who is it for?

Most people arrive with curiosity and an issue they want to understand better. It isn’t recommended for anyone currently in an acute mental health crisis or experiencing psychosis.
